IPS and EHDS Patient Summary: Key Differences

This document explains how the International Patient Summary and the EHDS/eHDSI Patient Summary relate to each other, and how those differences are represented in this dashboard.

Executive summary

The International Patient Summary (IPS) is a global, minimal and non-exhaustive patient summary intended to support unscheduled or cross-border care. The EHDS Patient Summary builds on the same broad clinical idea, but applies the European cross-border exchange context: eHDSI content expectations, EU validation rules, language and terminology requirements, and national infrastructure obligations.

In practical implementation terms, IPS is the portable clinical baseline. EHDS PS is the European exchange target that constrains, validates, and operationalises that baseline for use across Member States.

Purpose and operating context

Area IPS EHDS Patient Summary
Primary purpose Provide a compact, internationally understandable clinical summary for care. Enable regulated cross-border exchange of patient summaries within the European health data space.
Governance context International standards community specification. European policy, eHealth Network/eHDSI practice, national contact points, and EU interoperability expectations.
Implementation emphasis Common minimum data set and broad semantic interoperability. Conformance to European profiles, section rules, terminology handling, validation, and exchange workflow.
National adaptation Usually implemented as a general baseline with local extensions where needed. Expected to align European requirements with national identifiers, catalogues, services, and governance.

Content model

Both IPS and EHDS PS organise clinical information around the patient, a Composition, and clinically meaningful sections such as allergies, problems, procedures, medication, immunisations, results, devices, and social history. The difference is less about inventing a new kind of document and more about which sections are expected, how absence is represented, and how tightly the document is checked for exchange readiness.

The local alignment process used by this dashboard preserves source clinical facts and adds explicit empty sections only where the EHDS/eHDSI expectation requires a section to be present but the source bundle does not contain the clinical facts. For example, a missing Medical Devices section may be represented with an unavailable empty reason rather than inventing device data.

Terminology and localisation

IPS aims for internationally recognisable terminology so a receiving system can understand the summary even when it originates elsewhere. EHDS PS keeps that goal but adds a European operating layer: validation against selected EU profiles, controlled terminology expectations, translation/display needs, and national catalogue integration where appropriate.

In this project, the Irish enhanced bundle may include local medication catalogue enrichment and mapping evidence that is useful for Irish review. The Gazelle-facing EHDS copy is more conservative because validator profiles may reject local audit extensions or national codings that are not part of the selected validation profile.

Validation and exchange readiness

An IPS bundle can be structurally valid and still need additional work before it is suitable for EHDS/eHDSI exchange. EHDS readiness usually involves checking the selected European profiles, required or at-least-present sections, terminology bindings, document metadata, provenance, and the packaging rules expected by the receiving infrastructure.

The dashboard's differential view is therefore a review aid rather than a formal conformance statement. It shows what changed between the IPS, the EHDS validator-facing output, and the Irish fully enhanced output so reviewers can understand both clinical and technical changes before formal validation or sharing.

How to read this dashboard

  • IPS is the original patient summary bundle loaded from the source examples.
  • EHDS is the validator-facing European patient summary copy used for profile-oriented review.
  • Irish Fully Enhanced includes Irish alignment and enrichment evidence that may be useful for national review.
  • Differential View highlights sections and resources added, removed, or changed between those variants.
